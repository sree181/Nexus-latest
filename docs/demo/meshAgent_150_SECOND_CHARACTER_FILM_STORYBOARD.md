# meshAgent 150-Second Character-Led Product Film

**Working title:** The Change Before the Release
**Audience:** CISOs, engineering leaders, security operations teams, and enterprise technology buyers
**Format:** 16:9 master, 1920 × 1080, 30 frames per second
**Creative treatment:** Premium stylized 3D characters, accurate product-interface composites, restrained motion graphics, and a recurring thin teal evidence thread

**Production status:** Completed as a 150-second master, 30-second character teaser, and 15-second character website loop.

## Character continuity

**Maya** is a thoughtful South Asian developer in her early thirties, with shoulder-length softly wavy black hair, a teal blouse, dark navy cardigan, charcoal trousers, and a practical smart watch. **Daniel** is a calm Black security analyst in his late thirties, with close-cropped hair, a short beard, a white shirt, and a structured navy overshirt. **Elena** is a decisive Latina CISO in her mid-forties, with a sleek dark bob carrying a subtle silver streak, an ivory blouse, and a structured deep-teal blazer.

Their facial structure, hair, clothing, proportions, and voices remain consistent. The film avoids hacker stereotypes, robots, cascading code, and decorative graphs. The emotional tension comes from an approaching release and incomplete information.

## Visual and editorial rules

Use real product screenshots for all precise interface text. Generated character screens remain abstract until the verified UI is composited in post. Every transition follows the teal evidence thread from the original agent action through review, decision, exception, and verified remediation. Amber is limited to unresolved attention. Green appears only for demonstrated verification. The opening incident references are factual context; the Maya, Daniel, and Elena workflow is explicitly labelled as a fictional customer scenario.

The film should spend at least half its running time with the characters visibly present. Interface inserts may occupy a screen, desk display, or architectural panel, but should not replace the human story. Camera motion remains slow and physically plausible: dolly, restrained orbit, rack focus, and purposeful screen transitions.
